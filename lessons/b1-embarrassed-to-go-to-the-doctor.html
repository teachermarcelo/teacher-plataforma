01 · Warm-up

Why do people avoid difficult conversations?

Sometimes people feel embarrassed when they need to talk about a personal problem. They may worry about being judged, so they postpone asking for help. In this lesson, we will explore language for describing these feelings and ways to make conversations feel safer.

Learning goals / Objetivos
By the end of the lesson, you can explain why someone might delay an appointment, understand six useful expressions, use phrasal verbs in context, and discuss ways to make a person feel comfortable.

Before you begin

Think about a time when someone might feel nervous asking for help. You do not need to share private or medical information.

Listening strategy

First, listen for the main idea. Then listen again for details, reasons, and expressions that show how people feel.

Read the short adapted summary

A person who feels embarrassed about a health concern may put off making an appointment. This can mean that a problem is identified later than it could have been. A supportive professional can put the patient at ease by explaining what will happen, respecting privacy, and avoiding judgment. Preparing questions in advance or bringing a trusted person may also make a conversation easier.

04 · Grammar lab

Past Simple vs. Present Perfect

Use the Past Simple for a finished action at a definite time. Use the Present Perfect to connect a past experience or action with the present, when the exact time is not the focus.

Past Simple

Subject + past verb

She visited the doctor yesterday.

A ação aconteceu em um momento terminado: yesterday.

Present Perfect

Subject + have/has + past participle

She has visited the doctor before.

A experiência é relevante agora; o momento exato não foi informado.

Remember: “I delayed the appointment last week” (Past Simple) vs. “I have delayed the appointment before” (Present Perfect).
1. Choose the correct form: “The patient ______ the appointment yesterday.”
2. “I ______ never ______ so nervous before an appointment.”
3. “The doctor ______ to me ten minutes ago.”
4. “We ______ already ______ the information with the patient.”
05 · Speaking practice

Discuss, explain, respond

Answer in English. Try to give a reason and an example. You can use the prompts below to structure your answers.

Personal response · optional

1. Why might someone feel embarrassed when asking for help?

Useful language: “They might be worried that…”, “One reason could be…”, “They may feel uncomfortable because…”

Give advice

2. What could help a nervous person feel more comfortable during an important conversation?

Useful language: “It might help to…”, “A good idea would be to…”, “They could consider…”

Explain an expression

3. What does “suffer in silence” mean? Create a new example.

Useful language: “It means…”, “For example, someone might…”

Role-play

4. Student A is nervous about asking a personal question. Student B is a supportive professional. Practise a short, respectful conversation.

Useful language: “Please take your time.” “I’ll explain what happens next.” “You can ask me any questions.” “Your privacy matters.”

Critical thinking

5. How can facial expressions and tone of voice change the way a message is understood?

Useful language: “Even if the words are polite…”, “A calm tone can…”, “This may make the other person feel…”
